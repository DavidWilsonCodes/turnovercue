# Lead Generation Lab — Free Pilot Model

## Thesis

Lead generation can become a much stronger money engine than low-value microservices because the output is tied directly to a business's revenue.

But we should **not** collect consumer leads first and then casually sell their personal details to businesses.

Safer and cleaner first model:
1. obtain a business's permission for a free pilot;
2. advertise or build a funnel explicitly for that named business;
3. the business receives the leads generated for it;
4. after the trial, offer a paid continuation based on measured results.

This lets us learn without pretending we already have a mature agency.

## Why local services

A good lead-gen vertical has:
- high value per new customer;
- a clear local service area;
- simple qualification;
- visible buyer intent;
- enough margin to pay for leads;
- an owner who can answer/call leads quickly;
- no extremely sensitive/regulatory data for our first experiment.

Current advertising benchmarks support the economics of high-value lead categories.

2026 Meta lead-campaign benchmark CPL:
- Real Estate: US$13.74
- Home & Home Improvement: US$42.95
- Dentists & Dental Services: US$61.56

2025 Google Ads benchmark CPL:
- Automotive Repair/Service/Parts: US$28.50
- Home & Home Improvement: US$90.92
- Dentists & Dental Services: US$83.93
- Attorneys & Legal Services: US$131.63

These are broad US benchmarks, not expected Australian results. The point is that high-value businesses already tolerate meaningful acquisition costs.

## First-pilot vertical recommendation

**Roof repair / roof restoration / roofing quotes**

Why:
- high-ticket transaction relative to restaurants and commodity retail;
- highly visual creative possibilities;
- local service radius;
- straightforward quote-request qualification;
- no health information;
- less compliance complexity than medical/legal;
- job value can justify a meaningful lead value;
- service businesses often already understand paying for quote opportunities.

Alternates:
- tree removal / arborist
- concreting
- fencing
- pest control
- pool construction/renovation
- garage-door replacement
- premium landscaping
- painting / exterior restoration

Avoid as first pilot:
- dentists/medical: sensitive-health/privacy + ad-policy complexity;
- lawyers: expensive clicks + regulation;
- restaurants: low customer value despite cheap leads;
- financial services: regulation;
- emergency services requiring 24/7 lead response unless client is equipped.

## Pilot offer to the business

"Free 7-day lead-generation experiment. I am building and validating a new lead-generation system. I will create the campaign/funnel and manage the test. You keep every lead. There is no management fee for the pilot. We agree on what counts as a qualified lead before launch. If it works, I will show you the numbers and you can decide whether you want an ongoing paid arrangement."

Ad spend:
- preferable: business funds the ad spend directly in its own advertising account;
- alternate learning investment: we may fund a small capped test only with explicit approval.

Do not promise a number of leads.

## Qualification definition — example roofing

A qualified lead must:
- be located inside the agreed service area;
- provide a real first name;
- provide a valid phone/email;
- own/manage the property or be authorized to request the work;
- request one of the services the roofer actually provides;
- indicate an intention to obtain a quote.

Optional qualifying fields:
- suburb/postcode;
- roof issue/type;
- approximate property type;
- requested timeframe;
- upload photo.

Do not over-qualify a form until conversion data exists.

## Post-pilot monetisation choices

### Model A — management fee
A$300–A$1,000+ / month + client ad spend depending on scope and evidence.

### Model B — pay per qualified lead
Business pays an agreed amount for leads meeting the written qualification definition.

### Model C — pay per booked appointment
Higher price, but requires reliable booking/CRM confirmation.

### Model D — hybrid
Small base retainer + per-qualified-lead performance component.

Do not start with revenue-share on closed jobs. Attribution becomes messy and creates arguments over sales follow-up.

## Minimum proof required before selling aggressively

One pilot should demonstrate:
- ads/funnel launched correctly;
- genuine leads arrived;
- cost per lead known;
- lead quality feedback from the business;
- response-time/follow-up issues identified.

Three pilots in one vertical provide much stronger evidence.

## Consumer-data / Australian compliance guardrails

When collecting leads:
- clearly state who is collecting the information and why;
- state which business will receive/contact the lead;
- collect only fields needed for the quote;
- include appropriate privacy/consent language;
- do not reuse the lead for unrelated marketing;
- do not sell/share personal information outside what the person was told;
- respect opt-out/unsubscribe requirements for commercial electronic messages.

Australian Spam Act rules generally require consent for commercial email/SMS, sender identification/contact details and a working unsubscribe mechanism.

The Privacy Act can also become relevant to lead businesses. Importantly, some small businesses that trade in personal information may be covered even below the usual A$3 million turnover threshold.

Therefore the first pilot should be **business-first and consented**, not "generate a database of random consumers and shop it around later."

## First-business acquisition method

Do not mass-spam 500 businesses.

Find 10 suitable local businesses with:
- decent reviews;
- functioning website;
- visible capacity to take jobs;
- weak or inconsistent current advertising;
- owner/contact route;
- high-value service.

Approach individually.

Suggested message:

"Hi — I'm testing a new lead-generation process for [roofers] and looking for one local business to use as the free pilot. I would build the campaign/funnel and charge no management fee for the first 7 days. You keep every lead. Before anything goes live we'd agree on the service area and exactly what counts as a qualified enquiry. I'm doing this to prove the system with real results before offering it commercially. If it produces useful enquiries, I'll show you the numbers and you can decide whether continuing makes sense. Would you be open to seeing the pilot outline?"

No fake case study.
No claim of guaranteed results.

## Evidence ladder

Weak:
- business accepts pilot but no qualified leads.

Promising:
- 3+ legitimate qualified enquiries at a plausible CPL;
- business says lead quality is useful.

Strong:
- business voluntarily agrees to pay to continue;
- or second business accepts a paid pilot based on the first case study.

Money method counts toward 50:
**when a business pays for continued lead generation or pays for a lead/appointment.**
